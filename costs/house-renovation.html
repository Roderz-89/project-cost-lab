What moves the price

  • Scope creep. Once walls and floors are opened, damp, rot and old wiring often widen the job. Contingency matters most here.
  • Empty or lived in. Working in an empty house is faster and cheaper; living in means phasing the work.
  • Kitchen and bathroom spec. These two rooms can swing the total more than anything else.
  • Professional fees. A larger refurb often needs a designer or surveyor to plan and manage it.

Planning, Building Regulations and permissions

Internal refurbishment does not normally need planning permission, except for a listed building, which needs listed building consent for most changes. Rewiring, new heating, structural alterations, new drainage and replacement windows each fall under the Building Regulations, so use registered tradespeople who can self-certify, or notify Building Control. Check for asbestos before stripping out a house built before 2000.

General information for England unless stated, checked 15 November 2026. Rules differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland and change over time — confirm with your council, Building Control or the Planning Portal before you start.

Whole-house mid refurb cost FAQ

How much contingency should I hold on a renovation?

Older houses often justify 15–20% or more, because problems are only found once work starts. The calculator lets you set up to 25%.

Does the band include a new kitchen and bathroom?

A mid refurb band assumes an updated kitchen and bathroom at mid spec. Price them separately with their own projects if you want to see them on their own.

Is VAT lower on renovating an empty home?

A reduced 5% rate can apply to renovating a home that has been empty for two years or more, if the conditions are met. See our VAT guide and HMRC's guidance.
