A single-storey rear extension is the most common way to add space to a UK house. The band here covers foundations, walls, roof, windows and doors, insulation, first and second fix, plastering and decoration, as a shell-plus-finishes job at the chosen spec.

The number most people search for is a price per m², but fixed costs such as design, foundations near drains and knocking through to the house do not shrink with a smaller footprint. That is why small extensions cost more per m².

What moves the price

  • Glazing. Wide sliding or bi-fold doors and rooflights are often the biggest single upgrade. Tick the bi-fold extra to see the effect.
  • Steels and knock-through. Opening the back of the house needs structural steel; long spans need more.
  • Ground and drains. Building over or near a public sewer, clay soil or trees can mean deeper foundations and a build-over agreement with the water company.
  • The kitchen inside. Most extensions become kitchen-diners. The kitchen is an optional extra, not part of the shell.

Planning, Building Regulations and permissions

Many single-storey rear extensions in England can be built under permitted development within size limits; larger ones can use the prior approval route, and anything bigger, or in a conservation area, needs a householder planning application. Building Regulations approval is always needed, and work on or near a shared wall triggers the Party Wall Act. Check the current limits on the Planning Portal before you design, and see our planning fees post for the current fee.

General information for England unless stated, checked 15 November 2026. Rules differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland and change over time — confirm with your council, Building Control or the Planning Portal before you start.

Single-storey rear extension cost FAQ

What is a typical cost per m² for a single-storey extension?

Use the size table on this page: it divides each band by its floor area. The figure falls as the extension gets bigger because fixed costs are spread over more space.

Does the band include planning and design fees?

No. Planning and drawings, Party Wall and architect plus engineer fees are optional extras so you can see what each adds.

Should I add contingency?

For structural work a 10–15% contingency is a common planning allowance, more for older houses or unknown ground. The calculator applies it last.
