Re-roofing a pitched roof means stripping the old tiles or slates and battens, laying a new breathable membrane and battens, and re-covering, with new flashings and ridge and hip fixings. It is priced per m² of roof slope, which is larger than the house's floor plan because of the pitch.

Scaffolding is a large fixed part of any roof job and is included in the band for a typical house.

What moves the price

  • The covering. Concrete tiles sit at the budget end; clay tiles and natural slate push a job up.
  • Roof shape. Hips, valleys, dormers and chimneys all add cutting and leadwork.
  • Timber condition. Rotten rafters or sagging found once the roof is stripped are outside the band.
  • Insulation. Re-roofing is the moment to improve roof insulation, which may be required (see below).

Planning, Building Regulations and permissions

Replacing a roof covering like for like does not normally need planning permission, except on a listed building or in a conservation area with an Article 4 direction. Under the Building Regulations in England, renovating more than a quarter of a roof's area usually means notifying Building Control and upgrading insulation where it is practical. A roofer registered with a competent person scheme can self-certify this; otherwise check with Building Control.

General information for England unless stated, checked 15 November 2026. Rules differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland and change over time — confirm with your council, Building Control or the Planning Portal before you start.

Re-roof (pitched) cost FAQ

How do I measure roof slope area?

Roofers measure each slope's length up the pitch times its width. As a rough guide, a pitched roof's slope area is often a third to a half larger than the footprint of the house.

Does the band include scaffolding?

Scaffolding for a typical house is included. Extra hire time is an optional extra if the job overruns.

Is a flat roof priced the same?

No. A flat roof has its own project in the calculator, priced per m².
