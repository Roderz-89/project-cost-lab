What moves the price

  • Excavation and muck-away. Removing an old drive or lawn and taking spoil away is a large share of the cost.
  • Surface choice. Block paving takes more labour than tarmac; resin bound needs a sound base and skilled laying.
  • Drainage. Making the surface permeable, or adding a channel drain and soakaway, adds cost but may be needed (see below).
  • Extras. A dropped kerb, walls, gates and lighting sit outside the band.

Planning, Building Regulations and permissions

In England, paving a front garden with more than 5 m² of hard surface needs planning permission unless the surface is permeable or water drains to a lawn or border within the property. A dropped kerb onto the road always needs the council's permission and is usually built by an approved contractor, with its own fee. Check your council's current rules and fees.

General information for England unless stated, checked 15 November 2026. Rules differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland and change over time — confirm with your council, Building Control or the Planning Portal before you start.

Driveway cost FAQ

Is block paving or tarmac cheaper?

Tarmac is usually cheaper to lay; block paving costs more but is easier to repair in patches. Use the spec setting to compare.

Does the band include a dropped kerb?

No. A dropped kerb needs council permission and is priced separately by the council or its approved contractor.

Do I need planning permission for a driveway?

Not if the surface is permeable or drains within your property, in England. Otherwise, more than 5 m² of front garden paving needs planning permission.
