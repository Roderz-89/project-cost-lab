What moves the price

  • Material. White uPVC is the lowest-cost frame; coloured or woodgrain finishes, aluminium and timber cost more.
  • Glazing. Triple glazing, acoustic or safety glass and integral blinds add to each unit.
  • Shape and size. Bays, arched heads and floor-to-ceiling units need more frame and fitting time.
  • Access. Upper-floor windows above a conservatory or on a tall house may need scaffolding.

Planning, Building Regulations and permissions

Replacement windows in a house do not normally need planning permission if they look similar to the existing ones, but listed buildings, conservation areas with an Article 4 direction and flats can be different. Under the Building Regulations they must meet thermal and ventilation standards; an installer registered with a competent person scheme (such as FENSA or CERTASS) can self-certify and give you a certificate, which you will need when you sell.

General information for England unless stated, checked 15 November 2026. Rules differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland and change over time — confirm with your council, Building Control or the Planning Portal before you start.

Replacement windows cost FAQ

Is the price per window or per m²?

Per window, for a typical domestic size. Very large or shaped windows cost more than the band suggests.

Do I need a certificate for new windows?

Yes. Ask for the competent person scheme certificate or a Building Control completion certificate; buyers' solicitors will ask for it.

Are external doors included?

No. Front and back doors and bi-fold pairs have their own project in the calculator.
