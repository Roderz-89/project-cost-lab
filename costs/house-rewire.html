A full rewire replaces the cables, sockets, switches, light fittings' wiring and usually the consumer unit throughout a house. It is priced here per m² of internal floor, because the number of circuits and the length of cable grow with the house.

Most of the disruption comes from lifting floorboards and chasing walls, so a rewire is often timed with replastering and decorating.

What moves the price

  • Access. An empty house with floorboards up is far quicker to wire than a lived-in, carpeted, freshly decorated one.
  • Number of points. Extra sockets, outside lights, data cabling and EV-ready circuits all add time and materials.
  • Making good. Replastering chased walls and redecorating are separate jobs; the replaster and decorate projects have their own bands.
  • The consumer unit. Usually replaced as part of a rewire; it is also a separate project in the calculator.

Planning, Building Regulations and permissions

A rewire is notifiable electrical work under Part P of the Building Regulations in England and Wales. Use an electrician registered with a competent person scheme, who can self-certify the work and give you an Electrical Installation Certificate; otherwise Building Control must be notified before work starts. An Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) on the existing wiring helps decide whether a full rewire is needed.

General information for England unless stated, checked 15 November 2026. Rules differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland and change over time — confirm with your council, Building Control or the Planning Portal before you start.

Full house rewire cost FAQ

How do I know if a house needs rewiring?

An electrician's Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) will say. Old rubber or fabric cable, no earth to lighting circuits or an old fuse box are common signs.

Can I stay in the house during a rewire?

Many people do, room by room, but it takes longer and costs more than an empty house. The living-in setting reflects that.

Does the band include replastering?

No. The band covers cable, the consumer unit, sockets and switches, testing and certification. Replastering and decoration are separate projects in the calculator.
