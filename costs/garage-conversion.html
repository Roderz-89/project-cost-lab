What moves the price

  • The floor. Garage floors are usually lower than the house and uninsulated; raising and insulating them is a large share of the work.
  • The new wall. The infill under the old door needs a foundation that may not already exist; new foundations are outside the band.
  • Heating and services. Extending radiators or adding underfloor heating, and moving a boiler or meter if one sits in the garage, add to the band.
  • What the room becomes. A bedroom with an ensuite or a utility with plumbing costs more than a simple study.

Planning, Building Regulations and permissions

A garage conversion that changes only the inside and the door opening can often be done under permitted development in England. Some newer estates have planning conditions that remove those rights, so check the planning history for your house. Building Regulations approval is needed because a garage is not built to habitable standards for insulation, ventilation, fire safety and damp-proofing.

General information for England unless stated, checked 15 November 2026. Rules differ in Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland and change over time — confirm with your council, Building Control or the Planning Portal before you start.

Garage conversion cost FAQ

Do I need planning permission to convert a garage?

Often not, but some houses have permitted development rights removed by a planning condition. Check with your council or the planning history before you commit.

Is a detached garage priced the same?

No. A detached garage usually needs more work on the walls, roof and services. The calculator's garage conversion band is for an integral garage.

Will I lose parking?

Usually the garage space, and sometimes a council expects off-street parking to be kept. Check local parking standards if the driveway is short.
