Heat pumps in late 2026: the £7,500 grant, 0% VAT and the 31 March 2027 dates

Published 29 Oct 2026 · Last updated 29 Oct 2026

Update due. We will update this post when GOV.UK or Ofgem publish grant values for installations after 31 March 2027, and once the secondary legislation adding heat batteries to the 5% list is published.

Two dates matter for anyone pricing an air source heat pump in England or Wales this winter. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme (BUS) grant is £7,500 for an air-to-water heat pump, with a higher £9,000 available until 31 March 2027 for some off-gas-grid homes. And installed energy-saving materials, which include heat pumps, are zero-rated for VAT until 31 March 2027, with the rate returning to 5% from 1 April 2027. The Budget on 28 October 2026 confirmed that the VAT change will go ahead as planned. This post sets out the position as published, with a worked example from the Project Cost Lab calculator.

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme in late 2026

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme is run by Ofgem for the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ) and covers England and Wales. According to GOV.UK and Ofgem guidance, the grant values are:

  • £7,500 towards an air-to-water heat pump
  • £7,500 towards a ground source heat pump (including water source)
  • £2,500 towards an air-to-air heat pump
  • £5,000 towards a biomass boiler, where eligible

The government’s notice of approved grant categories sets a higher £9,000 for air-to-water and ground source heat pumps installed in off-gas-grid homes that replace oil or LPG heating, for installations from 21 July 2026 to 31 March 2027. Hybrid systems, where a heat pump works alongside a fossil fuel boiler, do not qualify.

The installer applies for the grant, not the homeowner, and must be certified under the Microgeneration Certification Scheme (MCS). The grant is taken off the price the installer charges. The scheme has its own conditions on the property, the existing heating system and insulation, which are set out on GOV.UK.

The Budget said the government will keep the Boiler Upgrade Scheme at current funding levels of £400 million in 2027-28, rising broadly in line with inflation after that. The Budget document does not set out grant values per installation beyond what is already published.

The VAT position

HMRC’s VAT Notice 708/6 explains that the installation of qualifying energy-saving materials in residential accommodation is zero-rated (0%) from 1 May 2023 to 31 March 2027. From 1 April 2027 the reduced rate of 5% applies. Heat pumps, solar panels and insulation are on the list. The Budget described this as reverting to the reduced rate “as planned”, and announced that heat batteries will be added to the list of qualifying materials at 5% from 1 April 2027, through secondary legislation.

The relief applies to the installation of qualifying materials. Other parts of a project, such as new radiators bought separately or general building work, can be standard-rated at 20%. HMRC’s notice explains how mixed supplies are treated. Our VAT on home works guide covers the general rules.

Running costs: what has changed on bills

A heat pump runs on electricity, so changes to electricity bills affect its running costs. DESNZ says VAT was removed from household electricity bills on 1 October 2026, and the Budget extended that cut until July 2027. The Budget expects this to take around £50 off the annual Ofgem price cap in April 2027.

The government has also asked Ofgem to remove standing charges from household gas bills, as early as 2027. DESNZ notes that the costs those charges pay for will move to the unit rate instead, so the effect on any single household depends on how much gas it uses. Electricity standing charges are also due to be reformed, with details to follow.

We have not estimated a running-cost saving here. Running costs depend on the home, the heat pump’s efficiency in practice, tariffs and how the heating is used. The boiler vs heat pump guide shows the arithmetic at the current price cap, so you can see how each input feeds through.

What to check on a quote

  • Which VAT rate has been applied, and to which items.
  • Whether the BUS grant has been deducted, and which grant value has been used.
  • Whether the installer is MCS certified, which is needed for the grant.
  • What enabling work is included, such as radiators, a hot water cylinder, pipework, controls and electrical work.
  • The expected installation date, given the 31 March 2027 dates above.

These are questions for comparing written quotes on the same scope. They are not a recommendation to install a heat pump, or to time a project around a VAT or grant date.
