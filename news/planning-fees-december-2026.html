Planning fees from 8 December 2026: £548 to £575 for a householder application

Published 6 Nov 2026 · Last updated 6 Nov 2026

Update due. We will update this post if the made regulations differ from the approved draft figures, and when MHCLG publishes the separate local fee-setting regulations.

From 8 December 2026, most planning application fees in England rise under a new national schedule. For a typical householder application — the enlargement, improvement or alteration of a single dwellinghouse — the fee moves from £548 to £575. Prior approval for a larger home extension or an additional storey rises from £249 to £310. Parliament approved the regulations on 27 October 2026. This post sets the new fees next to a Project Cost Lab extension and loft band so the change can be seen in scale.

What is changing on 8 December 2026

The Town and Country Planning (Fees for Applications, Deemed Applications, Requests and Site Visits) (England) (Amendment and Transitional Provision) Regulations 2026 amend the 2012 fees regulations. Both Houses of Parliament completed the affirmative procedure on 27 October 2026, and the instrument’s coming-into-force date is 8 December 2026. The Planning Portal’s forthcoming fee schedule sets out the current and new amounts side by side.

For homeowners, the figures that matter most are:

Application (England)Fee since 1 April 2026From 8 December 2026Change
Householder: enlargement, improvement or alteration of one dwellinghouse£548£575+£27
Outbuildings, gates, fences or walls within the garden (where charged separately)£272£285+£13
Prior approval: larger home extension (Part 1 Class A)£249£310+£61
Prior approval: additional storeys on a home (Part 1 Class AA)£249£310+£61
Discharge of conditions (householder)£89£125+£36
Vary or remove a householder condition (section 73)£89£112+£23
Non-material amendment (householder)£46£53+£7

MHCLG’s consultation response explains that the new national default schedule aims for about 90% cost recovery for local planning authorities. The rises are not a flat percentage: householder full applications move by about 5%, while some condition and prior-approval fees move by more.

When the new fee applies

The transitional rule is straightforward. An application made before 8 December 2026 pays the fee in force when it is made. An application made on or after that date pays the new fee. The trigger is the date of submission, not validation or determination. Annual inflation indexation of the new amounts does not restart until 1 April 2027, so there is no second national uplift in the first few months.

Submitting through the Planning Portal still attracts the Portal’s own service charge on top of the statutory fee. That charge is separate from the national planning fee and is set by the Portal, not by these regulations.

What is not changing

Listed building consent remains free of charge. Applications solely for works to adapt a dwelling for a disabled resident remain exempt. Tree works covered by a tree preservation order also stay outside the fee schedule. The “free go” for a repeat application after a refusal or withdrawal was already removed in earlier reforms and is not restored here.

These regulations set the national default only. The Planning and Infrastructure Act 2025 gives councils powers to set some fees locally later; MHCLG has said further regulations for local fee setting are intended before the end of 2026. Until those are in force, the national figures above are what apply.

With standard-rate VAT at 20% and a 10% contingency added, the extension band becomes £56,063 – £104,117 (mid £80,090) and the dormer band £54,794 – £101,760 (mid £78,277).

Statutory fee next to the mid: the householder fee rise of £27 is about 0.03% of the extension mid with VAT and contingency. The larger prior-approval rise of £61 is still under 0.1% of that mid. If a householder application also needs one discharge of conditions, the fee there rises by £36. Even stacking the householder fee, one prior approval and one condition discharge (£27 + £61 + £36 = £124) remains a small line next to a band that already spans more than £48,000 between low and high after VAT and contingency.

The calculator’s optional “Householder planning + drawings allowance” is a broader mid allowance of about £2,200 for drawings and related costs, not a live mirror of the statutory fee alone. We are not changing that allowance for a £27 national fee move; the fee change sits outside the compiled works band either way.

The width of the build band is the bigger number. Specification, ground conditions, structural steel, glazing and fit-out usually move a price far more than the planning fee. That is why the calculator shows a band rather than a single figure, and why the fee table above is best read as a fixed statutory charge, not as a share of the works.

Building control charges are a separate system

Planning fees are not building control fees. On 6 November 2026, The Building (Local Authority Charges and Amendments Relating to Charging) (England) Regulations 2026 (S.I. 2026/1094) came into force. They widen what local authorities in England may charge for building control functions and require published charging schemes, but they do not set a single national householder building-control fee. Those charges remain local. Confirm the fee with the local authority or registered building control approver for the property.

How to read this for a project budget

If a project needs a full householder application, budget £575 from 8 December 2026 for the national fee, plus any Portal service charge and any local pre-application advice fee the council publishes. If the work can go through permitted development with prior approval, budget £310 for that prior approval from the same date. Building Regulations approval, Party Wall matters and professional drawings are separate costs. Our extension guide and loft guide cover how the works bands are built; the calculator lets you tick a planning allowance if you want it inside the illustrated total.

Illustrative only. The worked example uses Project Cost Lab’s planning bands. It is not a quote, a valuation or advice to apply for permission, use permitted development or commission any work. Confirm the fee with the local planning authority or the Planning Portal on the day you apply, and get written quotes on the same scope before making decisions.
