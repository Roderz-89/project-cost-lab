How to use the ranges

Start with the calculator for a quick planning band, then use the relevant guide to understand what is driving it. A mid figure is a band, not a quote: the same headline project can move materially with structure, finishes, services and site constraints. Use the result to frame questions and compare written quotes based on the same scope, rather than treating it as a promise of what a contractor will charge.

These pages are deliberately practical rather than a specification or design service. They explain the assumptions behind the model, point out common omissions and show where a simple input can hide a complicated job. For examples of how different scopes sit alongside each other, browse the project catalogue as well as the topic guides below.

What each guide covers

Loft form variants. A basic Velux conversion usually preserves the roof shape, while a dormer adds usable headroom and changes the structural and external package. Hip-to-gable, L-shaped and mansard schemes can involve more roof rebuilding, steels, stairs, fire upgrades and finishes. The form matters more than calling every job a “loft conversion”, so compare like with like.

Why extension £/m² is blunt. Floor area is useful for a first pass, but it does not price kitchens, bathrooms, glazing, foundations, drainage, steels or tricky junctions. A compact, highly serviced room can cost more per m² than a larger simple shell. The guide explains how to use an area band without mistaking it for a tender.

Boiler, heat pump and PV. These are different packages, not interchangeable labels. A like-for-like boiler replacement may focus on plant and fluework; an air-source heat pump can require emitters, controls and external space; PV is a roof, inverter and electrical installation. Compare the scope, capacity and enabling work before comparing totals.

VAT as display, not ruling. The calculator’s VAT toggle shows how a band looks at selected rates or ex-VAT; it does not decide the correct treatment for a particular supply. Domestic works, qualifying energy measures, new dwellings and mixed scopes can have different facts. Check the current position with the supplier or a tax adviser.

Region and access uplifts. Labour markets and overheads vary around the UK, while parking, scaffolding, skips, crane access, long carries and restricted terraces can add cost anywhere. A regional factor is a planning adjustment, not a postcode price book. Record access constraints early and ask quotes to state what is included.
