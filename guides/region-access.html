Region and access — why the same drawing changes price

Last checked September 2026 · Published by Rodway Labs

Not a quote. Figures in this guide are illustrative UK cost bands compiled for planning. They are not a tender, valuation or building-regulation ruling. Get written quotes for the property. Project Cost Lab (Rodway Labs) is not a contractor, architect or quantity surveyor. The mid figure is a band, not a price; the VAT toggle is a display choice, not a ruling.

Two blunt levers in the model

Project Cost Lab applies regional and access factors on top of a compiled works mid. They exist because labour rates, scaffold logistics and parking differ across the UK — and because a rear extension on an open plot is not the same logistic job as the same footprint on a tight terrace with no side access. Both levers are illustrations, not a price book for your postcode.

Regional factors used in the calculator

  • UK mid-range — baseline
  • Inner London — about +30%
  • Outer London — about +22%
  • South East outside London — about +12%
  • East of England / South West — about +5%
  • West Midlands — mid
  • East Midlands — about −5%
  • North West / Scotland — about −7%
  • Yorkshire & Humber / Wales / Northern Ireland — about −10%
  • North East — about −12%

A contractor in a “cheaper” region can still price above the band if the specification is high or the programme is awkward. London premiums do not mean every borough quotes the same.

Access options

  • Easy — parking, skip space, side access for materials.
  • Typical suburban plot — the default assumption.
  • Tight terrace / no side access — more manual handling, scaffold complexity, neighbour coordination.

Access also interacts with occupation: living in during dusty or noisy works often slows the programme even when the pound figure looks similar.

Listed and conservation constraints

The listed / conservation uplift in the calculator (+15% when selected) is a blunt stand-in for extra design care and materials — not a heritage consultant’s fee quote. Planning risk still sits largely outside the band.

How to use these levers without fooling yourself

  1. Pick the region that matches where the property is, not where you wish rates were.
  2. Be honest about vans, skips and whether materials go through the house.
  3. Re-run the band when comparing a London quote with a regional one on the same drawing.
  4. Remember VAT display and contingency are separate knobs.
  5. Only written quotes settle the number — this lab sizes a conversation.
