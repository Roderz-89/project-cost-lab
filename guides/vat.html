VAT on home works — display choice, not a ruling

Last checked September 2026 · Published by Rodway Labs

Not a quote. Figures in this guide are illustrative UK cost bands compiled for planning. They are not a tender, valuation or building-regulation ruling. Get written quotes for the property. Project Cost Lab (Rodway Labs) is not a contractor, architect or quantity surveyor. The mid figure is a band, not a price; the VAT toggle is a display choice, not a ruling.

What this site does with VAT

The calculator includes a VAT control so you can view an illustrated band with 20% VAT, 5% VAT, 0% VAT, or ex-VAT. That control exists because homeowners compare quotes that present tax differently. It does not decide the rate that applies to your supplier, your property or your invoice.

Typical patterns (illustrative, not advice)

  • Most work on an existing dwelling is standard-rated in everyday domestic improvements — extensions, loft conversions, kitchens and bathrooms often appear with 20% VAT on labour and materials from VAT-registered contractors.
  • New dwellings and certain qualifying conversions can sit under different regimes. Whether your project qualifies is a facts-and-law question for the supplier and advisers — not for a web slider.
  • Some energy-saving measures may attract reduced or zero rates when conditions are met. Heat pumps, insulation and other measures are often discussed in that context; eligibility is not automatic.

Rodway Labs does not provide tax advice. If VAT treatment materially changes your budget, confirm it in writing on the quote.

Why the mid band can mislead if you ignore VAT

Comparing an ex-VAT shell quote with an inc-VAT turnkey quote is a classic way to think you have saved money when you have only changed presentation. Always ask: is the figure ex-VAT or inc-VAT, and which rate has the contractor applied?

In Project Cost Lab, pick the display that matches how you want to plan, then re-run when a quote states a different treatment. Contingency still sits on top of the illustrated total in the model.

What VAT does not fix

VAT treatment does not repair soft ground, Party Wall awards or under-specified glazing. Those remain outside or as optional allowances. Regional and access factors still apply to the works mid before tax display.

When VAT often applies to home works

For a typical improvement to an existing home, standard-rate VAT is the starting point when the contractor is VAT-registered. That can include labour and materials for a kitchen or bathroom refit, an extension, a loft conversion or other repairs. Improving energy efficiency or using an eco-labelled product does not by itself change the VAT treatment.

VAT is charged by the business supplying the work, so check who is contracting with you. A non-registered tradesperson may not add VAT, but that is not the same as the work qualifying for a zero or reduced rate. Ask for a clear total and explanation rather than inferring the rate from a materials list.

When a reduced or zero rate may be relevant

There are special rules for some new homes, qualifying conversions and certain work intended to improve energy efficiency or accessibility. Depending on the facts, the rules can cover the nature of the building, how it is occupied, the type of work, who supplies it and whether specific conditions or paperwork are met. A qualifying new dwelling is not the same as an extension to an existing dwelling, and an energy-saving product is not automatically enough on its own.

Treat phrases such as “5% VAT available” or “zero-rated” in an advert as a question to investigate, not as a conclusion. Ask the contractor to identify the basis and show it on the written quote or invoice. If eligibility depends on your household, building history, grant funding or a disability-related adaptation, check current HMRC guidance and ask an accountant or other suitably qualified adviser. Project Cost Lab is not giving a ruling.

How to use the calculator VAT toggle

Start with the project and region that best match the work, then open the Project Cost Lab calculator. The VAT control changes the way the illustrative cost band is displayed; it does not inspect your quote or decide whether you qualify. Use ex-VAT when comparing a contractor’s net figure, or choose an illustrative rate when you want a planning total that includes tax. For example, a £20,000 planning band shown at 20% becomes £24,000 before any contingency or project-specific allowances. The result is still a band, not a tender.

Keep like with like. If one quote includes VAT and another excludes it, use the toggle to understand the presentation, then ask suppliers to confirm their treatment. A VAT change can move the headline total without changing the scope or access difficulty. For wider assumptions, see all guides and regional and access factors.

Questions to ask before accepting a quote

  • Is the business VAT-registered, and is the quoted total ex-VAT or inc-VAT?
  • Which rate has been applied to the whole job, and are any items treated differently?
  • If a reduced or zero rate is claimed, what qualifying condition is being relied on?
  • Will variations, design changes and supplied materials be invoiced on the same basis?

Save the quote and eligibility evidence. For context, compare the loft conversion guide, extension guide and boiler, heat pump and PV guide; they also use planning bands, not promises about a supplier’s invoice.

VAT FAQ

Can I select 5% in the calculator because my project is energy-saving? No. The toggle is a display choice for scenarios. Confirm any reduced-rate eligibility with the supplier and HMRC or your accountant.

Practical checklist

  1. Ask every quote to state VAT rate and whether prices are ex or inc.
  2. Do not assume reduced rates for “eco” branding alone.
  3. Use this site’s VAT toggle only to align the planning band with the quote format.
  4. Keep a contingency even when VAT looks favourable.
