Combi swap versus heat pump versus PV

Last checked September 2026 · Published by Rodway Labs

Not a quote. Figures in this guide are illustrative UK cost bands compiled for planning. They are not a tender, valuation or building-regulation ruling. Get written quotes for the property. Same cupboard word, different job

Search results often lump “boiler cost”, “heat pump cost” and “solar panel cost” together as energy upgrades. On site they are different packages: a like-for-like combi swap in the same cupboard is not an air-source heat pump with emitters and controls, and neither is a roof-mounted PV array priced per kilowatt-peak. This guide separates the bands used in Project Cost Lab.

Gas boilers — compiled mid illustrations

A like-for-like combi in the same location is the cheaper domestic plant job in 2026 trade guides: compiled mid about £2,800 before VAT display and contingency. Relocation, flue changes and system cleans are common add-ons.

A system / regular boiler sits higher — compiled mid about £3,800 — because cylinders, open-vented pipework or heating-zone changes get involved. “Boiler” on an invoice can therefore mean either band.

Air-source heat pumps

An air-source heat pump for a typical house is a different order of work: outdoor unit, indoor cylinder or thermal store, controls, and often emitter upgrades if radiators were sized for hotter flow temperatures. This lab’s compiled mid is about £13,000 before grants and before your VAT display choice.

Grants and schemes sit outside the range. Do not net a government grant off inside this tool — treat support as a separate cash-flow item confirmed against current scheme rules. Fabric first (insulation, draughts) still affects whether a heat pump quote looks sensible.

Solar PV and batteries

Solar PV in this lab is priced per kWp (default 4 kWp, compiled mid about £1,650 / kWp before factors). Scaffolding, inverter type, roof covering and export arrangements change real quotes.

A battery is a separate line, typically per kWh of storage. Adding PV and a battery on the same day as a heat pump is three packages, not one “green” number.

Like-for-like swap or a system upgrade?

Start by naming the job accurately. A like-for-like combi swap normally keeps the boiler in the same position, reuses the existing heating layout and replaces the appliance, flue connection, filter and controls. It is still worth checking the flue route, condensate, isolation valves and system condition, but it is not the same scope as redesigning the heating system.

A system upgrade can mean changing from a combi to a system or regular boiler, adding a cylinder, separating zones or moving pipework. It may suit higher hot-water demand, water-pressure limits or an ageing cylinder. That is why a £2,800 combi mid is not a rival price for the same scope as a £3,800 system-boiler mid.

Heat-pump conversion is a wider retrofit. It needs an outdoor unit, suitable cylinder, lower-temperature heat delivery and controls that work with the building. Radiator sizing, pipework, electrical capacity and fabric improvements may follow. PV makes electricity; it does not replace a boiler or heat pump, while a battery stores electricity rather than hot water. They can work together, but their scopes differ.

Small details that move an energy quote

The cylinder is a common dividing line. A combi heats hot water on demand; a system or regular boiler normally works with a cylinder, as does a heat pump. Check that the quote includes the cylinder, valves, discharge pipework, controls and removal of the old arrangement; “boiler supplied and fitted” may not answer this.

Controls and emitters affect comfort and cost. A replacement boiler may need a thermostat or zoning; a heat pump needs weather compensation and a different operating pattern. Radiators may be adequate or unsuitable in some rooms. Retrofitting underfloor heating is a separate disruption and cost decision, not an automatic part of every heat-pump job.

Flue, access and the route through the house are practical cost drivers. A new or extended flue, core drilling, condensate drainage, scaffold, lifting an outdoor unit, long pipe runs and making good can change a simple-looking installation. Tight terraces, flats and parking restrictions affect labour. Use the region and access guide, then ask the installer to identify these items in writing.

Applying the calculator to a first budget

Use the calculator one package at a time. Choose Combi boiler like-for-like swap for the same position; select System / regular boiler replacement when cylinder work is included; choose Air-source heat pump for the wider retrofit. For PV, enter array size in kWp. A battery is separate and priced in usable kWh.

Test the knobs that describe your property. Specification is a broad equipment sensitivity, not a manufacturer selection. Choose region and access honestly: “tight terrace / no side access” matters where kit must be carried through the house or lifted, while clear delivery may be “easy”. Choose occupation when sequencing around living in. Add cylinder, underfloor-heating or scaffold only when it belongs to the package; do not force the result to resemble a quote.

Run a second scenario for a system upgrade and compare the scope, not just the mid. Record whether each result includes VAT, and keep grants outside until eligibility is confirmed. Boiler, heat pump and PV FAQ

Is a heat pump a direct replacement for a boiler? No. The heat source, temperatures, controls, cylinder and often emitters differ. A survey and design are needed before deciding whether the system can be adapted.

Does PV make a boiler free to run? No. Generation varies by daylight, season and roof orientation. Cost the array, inverter, battery and heating system as separate packages.

Why is my boiler quote above the mid? Relocation, flue work, cylinder or controls, access, remedial pipework, region and VAT may explain it. Ask for an itemised scope and compare like for like; the mid is not a target price.

Can it confirm VAT or a grant? No. VAT is a display choice, not a ruling, and grants are not netted off. Read the VAT on works guide and confirm both with the supplier or adviser.

VAT display on energy kit

Some energy-saving measures can attract reduced or zero VAT in UK rules, depending on the measure and the customer. That is why the calculator lets you show 20%, 5%, 0% or ex-VAT — as a display choice, not a determination. Confirm treatment with your installer and tax adviser. See the VAT on works guide.

How to compare quotes fairly

  1. Write down whether the job is like-for-like, relocation, full system or heat-pump retrofit.
  2. List emitters, cylinder, controls and electrical upgrades in or out.
  3. For PV, fix kWp, roof type and whether a battery is included.
  4. Ask each quote to state VAT treatment explicitly.
  5. Use this site only to size a planning band before those quotes arrive.
