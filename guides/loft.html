Loft conversion ranges: Velux to mansard

Last checked September 2026 · Compiled UK mid bands, before spec, region, VAT and extras · Published by Rodway Labs

Why form matters more than the word “loft”

Homeowners often search for a single “loft conversion cost”. In practice the envelope, steel, staircase and finishes decide the band. A rooflight conversion that keeps the existing ridge is a different job from a full mansard that rebuilds the roof plane. This lab prices lofts by useful floor area and stores separate compiled mid points for common UK forms.

Use the figures below to size a conversation with an architect or designer, then collect written quotes on the same drawing. Do not treat a mid point as a tender.

Compiled 2026 mid £ per m² (before factors)

  • Velux / rooflight — about £1,550 / m² (default example 22 m²). Least change to the roof form; headroom and staircase still drive cost.
  • Rear dormer — about £1,850 / m² (default 28 m²). Common suburban route when you need standing height at the eaves.
  • Hip-to-gable — about £2,050 / m². Extra masonry and roof work where a hipped end becomes a gable to unlock floor area.
  • L-shape dormer — about £2,150 / m². Two planes, more junctions, more weathering detail.
  • Mansard — about £2,400 / m². Heavier structural and roofing package; often the top of the domestic loft ladder in published guides.

These mid points sit before specification, region, access, occupation, listed uplift, VAT display and contingency in the calculator. Change those knobs and the illustrated band moves — sometimes by more than the gap between Velux and dormer.

Cost drivers by loft form

Velux / rooflight. The saving comes from retaining more of the existing roof structure, not from making the work simple. Rooflight size and number, trimming, insulation depth and the stair route all matter. Extensive strengthening or awkward head height can reduce the price advantage.

Rear dormer. A dormer adds a roof volume, so flashings, cheeks, covering and external finish join the weatherproofing package. Cost moves with width, cladding, windows, partitions and any bathroom. A larger dormer creates more usable area but also increases structure and finishes, rather than simply multiplying the headline £/m².

Hip-to-gable. This replaces a sloping hipped end with a vertical gable. Brickwork or render, temporary weathering, roof alterations and structural connections can be significant. It is often paired with a dormer, so model the combined scope rather than choosing the cheaper form in isolation.

L-shape dormer. The return creates useful space over a rear addition but introduces another roof junction, valley or abutment. These interfaces need careful structural and weatherproofing design. Rear access and the existing extension's condition can matter as much as nominal area.

Mansard. A mansard changes more of the roof, with a steep outer face, new structure and extensive coverings and insulation. Windows, parapets, fire details and bespoke finishes can push the total beyond a simple area calculation. Its higher band reflects the heavier envelope package.

Survey questions that move a loft band

Before comparing quotes, record ridge and eaves heights, the likely stair position and the locations of trusses, chimneys and services. Head height affects genuinely usable floor; low areas may remain storage. Stairs take room below and can change a landing, doors or ceilings. Discuss these design and Building Regulations matters with the relevant professional; the calculator cannot decide them.

Party walls and access are practical drivers. A shared wall, terraced layout or work affecting a neighbour may require notices or an agreement under the Party Wall etc. Act 1996. Narrow access, scaffolding, crane lifts, limited parking and an occupied home can add labour and protection. Check allowances in writing; this is not party-wall, planning or Building Control advice.

Planning status is property-specific. Some alterations may fall within permitted development limits, while others need an application; conservation areas, listed buildings and local restrictions can change the picture. Building Regulations approval is separate. Confirm the route with your local authority or appointed professional, not a calculator setting.

What usually sits inside the band

Typical published packages assume a weathertight conversion with insulation to current standards, plasterboard finishes, basic electrics and heating extensions, and a straightforward staircase. Exact inclusions vary by quote. In this lab, tick ensuite, a higher-spec staircase, planning, Party Wall or professional fees if those apply to your job — they are allowances, not measured bills of quantities.

What usually sits outside

Full roof rebuilds beyond the loft form, complex steel where trusses were never meant to be cut, asbestos removal, chimney stack issues, main-drainage diversions and temporary accommodation are classic band-breakers. Conservation-area or listed constraints can add design time and specialist materials; the listed slider is a blunt +15% illustration, not a heritage fee quote.

Headroom, stairs and Building Regulations

Useful floor area is not the same as the footprint of the loft void. Stairs eat space on the floor below; fire escape and insulation details affect both cost and programme. This site does not decide whether your loft qualifies under permitted development or needs a full application — that is a planning and Building Control matter for your property.

How to use the calculator for a loft

  1. Pick the form that matches the drawing (Velux through mansard).
  2. Set useful m², not the whole roof void.
  3. Choose spec, region and access honestly — tight terraces and Inner London move the band.

Loft conversion FAQ

Is a loft conversion cheaper than an extension?

It can be, but there is no universal winner. A loft avoids buying more garden footprint while an extension may have simpler access and fewer roof-structure changes. Compare like-for-like useful area, specification, access and professional fees rather than the form label alone.

Does a dormer always need planning permission?

No single answer applies to every property. Permitted development rights have limits and can be removed or affected by location, previous work and designation. Treat planning as a property-specific check, separate from Building Regulations, and confirm it before relying on a design or budget.

How much contingency should I allow?

Use the calculator's contingency as a planning sensitivity, not a promise. The right allowance depends on survey information, access, the age and condition of the roof, and how fixed the design is. Keep a separate record of exclusions and provisional sums when comparing written quotes.

Does the headline figure include VAT?

The published mid bands are before VAT and the VAT control is only a display choice. VAT treatment can depend on the work, property and supplier, so ask for the treatment to be stated on the quote and take specialist tax advice where needed. See the VAT guide for the calculator's assumptions.
