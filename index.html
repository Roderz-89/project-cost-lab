How to use the calculator

  1. Pick a project. Filter by category, then choose one of the 57 project types. Each has its own size unit — m² of floor, linear metres of kitchen run, a count of windows or radiators, or a whole job.
  2. Enter the size. Use the internal floor area or run length from a drawing or a tape measure. Sizes outside a project’s sensible range are held to its minimum or maximum.
  3. Set the factors. Choose region, specification (budget, mid or high), site access, whether you will live in during the works and whether the house is listed or in a conservation area.
  4. Tick the extras that apply. Professional fees, planning, Party Wall, steels, skips and similar items are shown as separate allowances so you can see what they add.
  5. Choose VAT and contingency. Pick the VAT display that fits your job (the VAT guide explains the rates) and a contingency of 0–25%. Then read the low, mid and high band, not just the mid.

How the range is worked out

Each project has a compiled mid rate per unit or per job. The calculator multiplies it by your size, then by specification, access, occupation, listed status and region. Low and high sit a set percentage either side of the mid (±22% to ±42%, wider for jobs that vary more, such as kitchens). Fixed extras are scaled by spec and region; fee extras are a percentage of the works. VAT is applied at the rate you choose, then contingency on top. The full factor list is in the methodology.

Edge cases to watch

  • Blank, zero or huge sizes: a blank or zero size falls back to the project’s default; very large sizes are held at the project maximum, so the band never runs away.
  • Energy-saving installs: heat pumps, insulation and solar panels are usually 0% VAT until 31 March 2027, so choose the 0% option rather than 20%.
  • Grants: the band is the cost of the works; any grant, such as the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, comes off afterwards — see the boiler and heat pump guide.
  • Hidden problems: asbestos, poor ground, rotten timbers or a Party Wall dispute can push a real cost outside the band entirely.

Calculator FAQ

Is the mid figure a quote?

No. It is an illustrative planning figure. Use it to size a budget, then get written quotes on the same drawing.

Why is my builder’s quote outside the band?

Your survey, design, local market and the contractor’s workload are not visible to the calculator. A quote outside the band is not automatically wrong — ask what it includes.

Should I add contingency?

For most structural work 10–15% is a common planning allowance; older houses and unknown ground often justify more.

How current are the figures?

The bands were compiled in September 2026 and are reviewed under the editorial policy. There is no live price feed.
