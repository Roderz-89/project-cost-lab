About Project Cost Lab

Last checked 14 Oct 2026

Project Cost Lab is an independent informational site from Rodway Labs in the United Kingdom. It shows how common home-project budgets move when you change size, specification, and site conditions. It is not a practice, a showroom, or a quote engine, and it does not sell building work.

What the lab is

The lab publishes a calculator and a short set of guides for jobs people in the UK actually try to price: lofts, extensions, conversions, kitchens and bathrooms, roofs and windows, gardens and drives, heat and energy, electrics and plumbing, decoration, and whole-house work. You pick a project type, set a quantity where the job is sized that way, and read a low, mid, and high figure. The mid figure is a planning band, not a price you can hand to a builder as if it were an offer.

There is no portfolio of finished jobs, no claim that anyone here designs houses, and no list of preferred contractors. We do not inspect properties, appoint trades, administer contracts, or issue tenders. The pages are written so a household can see which levers move an illustration before they ask for written quotes.

How the bands are compiled

The lab compiles illustrative 2026 UK ranges and then lets you edit the factors. Each project starts from a works mid for a stated unit — a square metre of extension, a linear metre of kitchen, a count of windows, or a whole job — with a spread either side so the low and high are not a single number pretending to be exact. Those anchors are editorial illustrations for planning. They are not a measured bill for a named house, and they are not a price book tied to your postcode.

Editable factors then sit on that works mid. Region scales the illustration from Inner London down to the North East. Specification is budget, mid, or high. Site access distinguishes an easy plot from a typical suburban one and from a tight terrace. A further factor covers living in the house during the works. A listed or conservation toggle is a blunt uplift, not a heritage fee. Optional extras are either a mid allowance, such as an extra scaffolding period or an extra skip, or a percentage of the works, and they are labelled as allowances. You choose a VAT display and a contingency percentage yourself.

Because those factors are editable, two people looking at the same project name can land in different bands without either result being a quote. That is intentional. The project name matters less than size, specification, access, occupation, and where in the UK the house stands. Ranges on the site were checked as illustrations in September 2026; they are not updated from live tender returns.

Editorial policy

Who writes and checks. All text, figures and calculator logic are written and checked by Rodway Labs. There are no guest authors, sponsored pages or paid placements, and no contractor, supplier or advertiser sees or approves content before it is published.

Sources. Cost bands are editorial compilations (see the methodology below). Official rules — VAT, grants, permitted development and Building Regulations — are checked against gov.uk, Ofgem or the Planning Portal and linked on the page where they are used.

Review cadence. We aim to review the price bands about once a year, and to re-check official-rule sections when the underlying guidance changes. A page’s “Last reviewed” date only changes when that page has actually been re-read and checked.

Corrections. Report an error via the contact page; confirmed corrections are made on the page concerned.

Methodology

Where the bands come from. The 57 project types and their mid points were compiled editorially by Rodway Labs in September 2026 from published UK trade and consumer cost guides for 2026. The data file does not record which guide each figure came from, so we do not claim named sources, survey data or live tender prices. Official rules quoted in the guides (VAT, the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, permitted development, Building Regulations) are linked to gov.uk, Ofgem or the Planning Portal on the page.

How the mids and spreads were set. Each project has one mid point: a rate per unit (per m², per linear metre or per item) multiplied by your size, or a whole-job figure. The low and high are the mid minus and plus a set spread, between ±22% and ±42% depending on how variable the job is (wider for kitchens and bathrooms, narrower for whole-house work). Sizes are kept within each project’s sensible minimum and maximum.

How region, access and spec work. The works mid is multiplied by: specification (budget ×0.78, mid ×1, high ×1.38); access (easy ×1, typical suburban ×1.06, tight terrace ×1.16); living in during works (×1.08); the listed or conservation toggle (×1.15); and region (UK mid-range ×1; Inner London ×1.3; Outer London ×1.22; South East (ex-London) ×1.12; East of England ×1.05; South West ×1.05; West Midlands ×1; East Midlands ×0.95; North West ×0.93; Yorkshire & Humber ×0.9; North East ×0.88; Wales ×0.9; Scotland ×0.93; Northern Ireland ×0.9). Fixed-price extras are scaled by specification and region; professional fees are a percentage of the works. VAT is applied at the rate you choose, then contingency on top.

Review cadence. The price bands were compiled in September 2026, and the guides were last reviewed on 14 Oct 2026. We aim to re-check the bands about once a year, and to re-check the official-rule sections whenever the gov.uk, Ofgem or Planning Portal guidance changes. There is no live price feed.

Limits. The factors are broad multipliers, not a postcode price book. The bands do not see your survey, ground conditions, structure, drawings or contractor market, and they are not a quote, valuation or professional advice. Use them to size a budget, then compare written quotes on the same drawing.

What it is not

Rodway Labs is not a contractor, architect, or quantity surveyor. The site does not hold itself out as a professional appointment, and it does not offer design, contract administration, or a valuation. A band that happens to sit near a quote you received is a coincidence of inputs, not confirmation that the quote is fair. Ground conditions, asbestos, rotten joists, a long drain run, a Party Wall award, or a planning refusal can sit outside the band entirely.

The VAT control shows a band at the rate you choose; the VAT guide explains which rate usually applies. Nothing on the site is tax, legal or building-regulations advice.

How the guides fit

The guides explain the levers the calculator uses, in ordinary language: how extension area is discussed, what a loft range is trying to show, how to read a boiler or heat comparison, what the VAT display does and does not decide, and how region and access change a band. Read the relevant guide before you treat a mid figure as a budget you would defend to anyone else. Then get written quotes for the property on the same drawing.

If a page is unclear, or a factor on the site no longer matches the guide that describes it, use contact. That address is for a correction or a question about the method. It is not a request line for a price, a survey, or a contractor recommendation. The disclaimer sets the limit on what any figure here can be used for.
